About the Sharma ABS model

Front–back tilt

Pack the bottom hinge out by 3–6 mm toward the swing side. The pull is strongest near closed, so the door latches well, but it won't come back once opened past 90°.

Side-wise tilt

Shim the top hinge so the axis leans toward the latch side. The door rises whichever way it opens, which suits double-acting swing doors, but the pull is weak right at closed.

Weight and friction

Gravity pull and hinge friction both grow with door weight, so a heavy door closes about as reliably as a light one. The hinge type decides it. Heavy doors need bearing hinges or a floor pivot.

How accurate is this?

The gravity geometry and door inertia (uniform panel, 0.9 m × 2.0 m) are exact. Hinge friction uses a Coulomb stick–slip model with typical textbook friction coefficients and 6 mm pin and bearing radii, not measured values. Latches, rubber seals, door warp and hinge play are not modelled, and air drag is a single estimated constant.

Use it to choose the tilt direction and roughly how much, then fine-tune on the real door. To calibrate, hang a spring balance at the handle and note the force at which the door just starts to move.
